Law firm SEO is the work of getting your firm found when people search for a lawyer, both in Google's results and in the answers AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity give. In 2026 that means two jobs at once: rank in the Google map pack and organic results for your practice areas and cities, and become one of the firms AI answers name when someone asks "who is a good injury lawyer near me?" The second job depends heavily on what legal directories, review sites and other third parties say about you, not only on your own website.

This guide is for managing partners, marketing leads and solo attorneys in the US. It covers what has changed, what has not, and a practical order of work.

## What Is Law Firm SEO?

Law firm SEO is search engine optimization for legal practices. It covers three areas:

- **Local SEO:** your Google Business Profile, reviews and directory listings, which decide whether you show up in the map pack for "[practice area] lawyer near me" searches.
- **On-site SEO:** practice area pages, attorney bio pages, location pages and articles that answer the questions prospective clients actually type.
- **Off-site reputation:** links, mentions and profiles on legal directories, news sites and bar association pages.

What makes legal different from most industries is the combination of high case values, intense local competition and strict advertising rules. A single signed case can be worth more than a year of SEO fees, so competition for terms like "car accident lawyer [city]" is fierce. At the same time, every page you publish is attorney advertising and has to follow your state bar's rules.

## How Has AI Search Changed Law Firm SEO?

People now ask AI assistants for recommendations the way they used to ask a friend. "Who's the best divorce lawyer in Tampa?" typed into ChatGPT returns a short list of named firms, often with a sentence on why each one made the cut.

We tested this directly. In [our ChatGPT lawyer recommendation experiment](/blog/chatgpt-recommendation-experiment/), we asked for personal injury firm recommendations across five US cities, three separate runs per city. Two findings matter for any firm:

1. **The shortlists were stable.** The same local firms came back run after run. If you are on the list, you tend to stay there. If you are not, you do not appear by chance.
2. **Not one answer cited a firm's own website.** Every shortlist was assembled from third-party sources such as Forbes, Best Lawyers, Expertise, SuperLawyers and Chambers.

That second point changes where your effort should go. Your website still matters: it is where Google ranks you, where AI tools land when they do read your pages, and where a prospect decides to call. But the decision about *whether you get considered* is increasingly made on pages you do not own.

What has not changed is just as important. AI assistants and Google's AI features still lean on the same signals that drove traditional local SEO: consistent business information, real reviews, clear answers and a reputation that shows up in more than one place. Firms that did local SEO properly are already ahead. Firms that relied only on paid ads and a pretty homepage are the ones losing ground.

## How Does Google Rank Law Firms in the Map Pack?

For local searches, Google weighs three factors, summarized in [Ahrefs' guide to SEO for lawyers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-for-lawyers/):

| Factor | What it means | What you can control |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well your profile matches the meaning of the search | Primary and secondary categories, services, practice area descriptions |
| Distance | How far your office is from the searcher or the location in the query | Office locations (honestly listed), location pages on your site |
| Prominence | How well known your firm is, online and offline | Reviews, links, directory profiles, press mentions |

You cannot fake distance, and the same Ahrefs guide is blunt that nobody can guarantee a map pack position. Relevance and prominence are where the work goes.

### Get the Google Business Profile right

Your profile is the single most visible asset in local search. For each office:

- Choose the most specific primary category that fits ("Personal injury attorney" rather than "Lawyer" if that is your main practice), then add secondary categories for other practice areas.
- Use your real firm name. Stuffing keywords or cities into the business name breaks Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended.
- List every service you actually offer, with a short plain-language description.
- Add real photos of the office, the team and the building entrance.
- Keep hours accurate, including holiday hours, and say whether you offer free consultations if you do.

Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/) walks through each field, verification and the common reasons profiles get suspended.

### Build review depth, not just a high score

In our experiment, a high star rating on a handful of reviews did little. The firms that kept appearing had a strong average across a large number of reviews, built over time. Ask every satisfied client, make it easy with a direct review link, and reply to every review, positive or negative, without revealing anything confidential about the matter.

Never buy reviews, offer anything in exchange for them, or write them yourself. Beyond platform rules, many state bars treat this as misleading advertising.

## Which Directories and Third-Party Sources Matter Most?

Because AI answers in our test drew entirely on third-party sources, your directory presence is now a ranking asset in two places at once: Google and AI assistants.

Start with the sources that show up for your own practice area and city. A simple way to find them:

1. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Mode the question your clients would ask, for example "best estate planning attorney in Austin", three or four times each.
2. Write down every source each answer cites or names.
3. Combine that list with the standard legal directories. Ahrefs' lawyer guide names [FindLaw, Justia and LegalMatch](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-for-lawyers/) as common ones; in our experiment the cited sources were Forbes, Best Lawyers, Expertise, SuperLawyers and Chambers.
4. Audit your firm and each attorney on every source: is the profile there, is it complete, and do the name, address and phone number match your Google profile exactly?

Our guide to [checking whether ChatGPT recommends your brand](/blog/check-if-chatgpt-recommends-your-brand/) has a 15-minute version of this audit you can run today. If you want it tracked across engines every month, that is what [Rank on AI](/rank-on-ai/) does.

Peer-reviewed rankings and editorial lists (for example Best Lawyers or Chambers) are not something you can buy your way onto, which is exactly why AI systems treat them as credible. Treat them as a long-term reputation project, not an SEO tactic.

## What Should a Law Firm Website Include?

Your site has to serve three readers: Google's crawler, AI systems that read and quote pages, and a stressed person deciding whether to call you. The same structure works for all three.

### Practice area pages

Give every practice area its own page, and split broad ones. "Personal injury" is a hub; "truck accident", "motorcycle accident" and "slip and fall" deserve their own pages if you take those cases. Each page should:

- Open with a two- or three-sentence answer to the main question ("Do I need a lawyer after a truck accident in Georgia?").
- Explain how the process works in your state, step by step.
- List the documents or information a client should gather.
- Say plainly what you do and do not handle.
- End with a short FAQ using the questions you hear in intake calls.

The opening answer matters most for AI search. Systems that build answers from web pages prefer passages that answer the question directly and define their terms. Our guide to [getting cited in Google AI Overviews](/blog/get-cited-in-google-ai-overviews/) covers this pattern in more detail.

### Attorney bio pages

AI answers often recommend people, not just firms. Each attorney should have a dedicated page with bar admissions, education, practice focus, notable published matters (where your state's rules allow you to mention them), speaking and writing, and links to their directory profiles. Use the same name format everywhere so search engines and AI tools can connect the profiles.

### Location pages

If you have more than one office, give each a page with the address, phone number, map, parking or access notes, and the attorneys who work there. Do not create pages for cities where you have no office and no real connection. Thin "city + practice area" pages written only to rank rarely work and can look misleading to both Google and a state bar.

### Structured data

Mark up each office with the schema.org `LegalService` type (a kind of `LocalBusiness`), using the same name, address, phone and hours as your Google profile. Add `Person` markup on attorney pages. Structured data does not guarantee rankings, but it removes guesswork about who you are and where you practice.

### Technical basics

Legal sites are often built on heavy themes with slow pages, duplicate practice pages and broken intake forms. A [technical SEO audit](/blog/technical-seo-audit-checklist/) once or twice a year catches most of it. You can get a quick read on your own site with our free [SEO checker](/seo-checker/).

## What Advertising Rules Apply to Law Firm SEO?

This article is not legal advice, and the rules differ by state. Still, every law firm marketer should know the basics:

- **No false or misleading claims.** ABA Model Rule 7.1, which most states have adopted in some form, prohibits false or misleading communications about a lawyer's services. That covers blog posts, practice pages, directory profiles and anything an agency writes for you.
- **Careful with "specialist" and "expert".** Many states limit when a lawyer can claim to be a specialist, often tying it to formal certification.
- **Past results need context.** Publishing verdicts and settlements is allowed in many states, but often only with disclaimers that past results do not guarantee future outcomes.
- **Testimonials and reviews** may carry their own state-specific requirements.

Two practical habits keep you safe. First, have an attorney who knows your state's rules review new pages before they go live, including anything written with AI tools. Second, keep claims specific and checkable. That is also what AI systems repeat with confidence, so compliance and visibility point in the same direction.

## How Long Does Law Firm SEO Take?

Expect months, not weeks. An [Ahrefs study cited in its lawyer guide](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-for-lawyers/) found it takes three to six months on average to start seeing results from SEO. In competitive practice areas and big metros, reaching the top of the map pack can take longer.

Some work pays back faster. Fixing a miscategorized Google profile, correcting inconsistent directory listings and adding missing practice pages can move results within weeks. Review depth, editorial recognition and links build more slowly.

For a sense of what a focused local program can produce, one of our clients, a mobile auto-glass shop in Charlotte, gained 1,747 Google Business Profile views, 82 website clicks and 71 phone calls from the profile in its first 90 days, alongside 6 new local landing pages and 114 technical fixes. ## Law Firm SEO Checklist for 2026

Work through this in order. Each step builds on the one before it.

1. Claim and verify a Google Business Profile for every real office, with the most specific category.
2. Make your firm name, address and phone number identical on your site, Google and every directory.
3. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Mode your clients' top questions and record the sources they cite.
4. Create or complete profiles for the firm and each attorney on those sources and the main legal directories.
5. Set up a steady review request process and reply to every review.
6. Build one page per practice area, each opening with a direct answer and ending with an FAQ.
7. Publish full attorney bio pages and link them to the matching directory profiles.
8. Add `LegalService` and `Person` structured data.
9. Fix technical issues: speed, mobile layout, broken forms, duplicate pages.
10. Have every new page reviewed against your state bar's advertising rules.
11. Re-run your AI prompts and check map pack positions every month.

## FAQ

### Is SEO worth it for a law firm?

For most firms, yes, because a single signed case can cover a long stretch of SEO costs and organic leads keep coming without paying per click. It works best for firms that can commit for at least six months and have the capacity to handle more intake calls. Firms that need cases next week usually pair SEO with paid search while organic visibility builds.

### How do I get my law firm recommended by ChatGPT?

Be present, complete and consistent on the sources AI answers draw from, which for legal are mostly directories, peer rankings and review platforms. Build review depth, publish specific and checkable information about your attorneys and practice areas, and re-check your visibility monthly. In our own test, no AI answer cited a firm's website, so off-site work matters more than many firms expect.

### What is the most important ranking factor for law firms in Google Maps?

Google weighs relevance, distance and prominence together, so no single factor wins on its own. For most firms the biggest controllable levers are a well-categorized Google Business Profile and a steady flow of genuine reviews. Distance is fixed by where your office actually is.

### Can we use AI to write our law firm's website content?

You can use AI tools for drafts, but every page needs review by someone who knows the law and your state's advertising rules. AI drafts tend to be generic and can include confident errors, which is a compliance risk for a law firm. Add your own process details, local specifics and attorney insight before publishing.

### Should every attorney have their own Google Business Profile?

It depends on your firm's structure and Google's rules for individual practitioners, which treat solo practitioners differently from attorneys at a multi-lawyer firm. Separate attorney listings can compete with the firm's main profile, so decide deliberately. In any case, every attorney should have a complete bio page on your site and consistent directory profiles.
