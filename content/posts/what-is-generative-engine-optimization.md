Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the practice of shaping your content so that AI answer engines such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews find it, trust it and cite it when they write an answer. Classic SEO aims to earn a ranked link and a click. GEO aims to earn a mention or a citation inside the generated answer itself.

The term is not marketing fluff invented by agencies. It comes from an academic paper, and the practical work behind it overlaps heavily with good SEO. This guide explains what GEO is, how it compares with SEO and AEO, how AI engines pick their sources, and a short checklist you can start on this week.

## What Does GEO Mean?

GEO stands for generative engine optimization. A generative engine is any search or assistant product that reads several sources and writes a synthesized answer, usually with links or named sources attached. ChatGPT with search, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode all fit that description.

The name was introduced in the 2023 research paper [GEO: Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) by Pranjal Aggarwal and colleagues, later published at the ACM KDD 2024 conference. The authors framed GEO as a way for content creators to improve their visibility in generative engine responses and tested a set of content changes on a benchmark of queries.

In plain terms, GEO asks a different question from SEO:

- **SEO:** "Does my page rank for this query?"
- **GEO:** "When an AI answers this question, does it use my page, and does it name my brand?"

## How Is GEO Different From SEO and AEO?

The three terms describe overlapping work with different targets. Industry usage is not standardized, so treat the table as a working map rather than a rulebook.

| | SEO | AEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full name | Search engine optimization | Answer engine optimization | Generative engine optimization |
| Target | A ranked link in search results | A direct answer, featured snippet or voice response | A citation or brand mention inside an AI-written answer |
| Where it shows up | Blue links on Google, Bing | Featured snippets, People Also Ask, voice assistants | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews, AI Mode |
| Main levers | Relevance, links, technical health, intent match | Concise answers, question headings, structured data | Clear answer blocks, topical depth, third-party mentions, crawlability |
| How you measure it | Rankings, clicks, impressions | Snippet ownership, answer-box presence | Citations and brand mentions across a set of prompts |

Many practitioners argue that AEO and GEO describe nearly the same job, and that both sit on top of SEO. We agree with the layered view. A page that cannot be crawled or indexed will not be cited by anything, so SEO is the foundation. GEO is the layer that decides whether the answer includes you.

## Is GEO Replacing SEO?

No. Google's own documentation on [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says a page needs to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet to appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, with no additional technical requirements. That is an SEO baseline.

What changes is the outcome you are chasing. A user can get a full answer without clicking, so being named in the answer becomes part of the job alongside earning the click. We cover the Google side in detail in [how to get cited in Google AI Overviews](/blog/get-cited-in-google-ai-overviews/).

## How Do AI Engines Choose Which Sources to Cite?

Each engine works differently, and none of them publishes a full recipe. Three mechanics come up repeatedly and are worth designing for.

### They break a question into smaller searches

Google describes AI Overviews and AI Mode as using a "query fan-out" technique: the system issues several related searches across subtopics and then assembles the answer from what it finds. A page that answers one narrow sub-question well can be pulled in even when it does not rank first for the broad term. Our explainer on [query fan-out in AI search](/blog/query-fan-out-ai-search/) shows how to map those sub-questions.

### They prefer passages that stand on their own

An engine needs a block of text it can quote without rebuilding context. A definition, a numbered process or a comparison table that makes sense in isolation is easier to use than an argument spread over four paragraphs.

### They lean on sources beyond your own site

When we tested what ChatGPT recommends in a competitive category, the same few names kept coming up, which suggests that broader web presence matters, not only on-page content. The write-up is in [our ChatGPT recommendation experiment](/blog/chatgpt-recommendation-experiment/). Directories, review sites, reputable publications and community discussions can all reinforce whether a brand looks real and relevant.

## What Did the GEO Research Actually Test?

The original paper evaluated strategies such as adding statistics, adding quotations from credible sources, citing sources and improving fluency, and compared how each changed a page's visibility in generative engine answers. Two cautions apply when you read summaries of it:

- **It is one benchmark.** The results come from the authors' test setup. Real engines change their behavior often, so treat the findings as directional.
- **Be wary of secondhand numbers.** Headline percentages get repeated by marketing blogs without the context. Read the [paper itself](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) before you quote a figure.

The useful takeaway is the direction: content that is specific, sourced and easy to extract tends to be more useful to a generative engine than content that is vague.

## What Should You Do Today? A GEO Checklist

Start with the foundation, then work down. Each step is small enough to finish in an afternoon.

1. **Confirm the basics.** Your key pages are indexed, return 200, have a canonical tag and are not blocked in robots.txt. Check that AI crawlers you want, such as PerplexityBot, are not blocked either.
2. **Open with the answer.** Put a direct two-sentence answer at the top of each page and under each major heading.
3. **Write headings as questions.** "How do I..." and "What is..." headings match how people prompt AI tools.
4. **Add one extractable asset per page.** A comparison table, a numbered checklist or a clear definition list.
5. **Source your claims.** Link every statistic to its origin. Cut numbers you cannot source.
6. **Cover the sub-questions.** List the follow-ups a buyer would ask and answer each in its own section.
7. **Build mentions elsewhere.** Make sure your brand and what it does are described accurately on directories, profiles and relevant third-party articles.
8. **Add an FAQ.** Short questions with 2-4 sentence answers are easy for engines to lift.
9. **Test with real prompts.** Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini the questions your customers ask, and record who gets named.

## How Do You Measure GEO?

There is no Search Console report for AI answers, so measurement is manual or tool-assisted. Keep it simple:

- **Prompt set.** Pick 15 to 25 prompts that match your buying questions.
- **Share of mentions.** For each engine, record whether your brand is named, cited with a link, or absent.
- **Competitors.** Note which brands and sources appear instead of you.
- **Trend.** Re-run monthly and compare. Answers vary between runs, so look for patterns over several runs rather than single results.
- **Referral traffic.** Check analytics for visits from AI domains, and treat it as a partial signal because many AI answers send no click.

## Common GEO Mistakes

- **Treating it as separate from SEO.** If pages are not indexed or are slow and broken, GEO work is wasted.
- **Chasing a single magic tactic.** No one factor guarantees a citation.
- **Publishing unsourced statistics.** Engines and readers both favor claims they can trace.
- **Stuffing the page with keywords or "AI-optimized" phrases.** Clear writing beats formulaic wording.
- **Measuring once.** AI answers shift between runs and over time, so one check proves little.

## FAQ

### Is GEO the same as AEO?

They overlap a lot. AEO usually refers to winning direct answers such as featured snippets and voice results, while GEO refers to being cited in longer AI-generated answers. In practice, the same habits serve both: clear answers, sourced claims and structured pages.

### Do I need special markup or an llms.txt file for GEO?

Google states that AI features have no additional technical requirements beyond being indexed and snippet-eligible. Other engines publish less detail. Standard structured data helps machines understand a page, but it is not a guaranteed route to citations. Our explainer on [what llms.txt is and who actually reads it](/blog/llms-txt-explained/) covers that file in detail.

### Does GEO work for small businesses?

Yes, and local and niche businesses can benefit because AI answers often cover specific questions where few strong sources exist. Accurate listings, clear service pages and consistent mentions across the web are the practical starting points.

### How long does GEO take to show results?

There is no reliable timeline. Changes to your own pages can be picked up once they are recrawled, while third-party mentions and brand recognition build slowly. Track your prompt set monthly and judge the trend rather than a single week.

### Can I track how often AI tools mention my brand?

Yes. You can test a fixed set of prompts manually, or use a monitoring tool. Record the engine, the prompt, the date and which brands were named so you can compare runs.
