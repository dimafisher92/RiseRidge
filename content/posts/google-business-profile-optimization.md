Google Business Profile optimization is the work of making your free Google listing complete, accurate and trustworthy so it appears for local searches and Maps. The steps that matter most are claiming and verifying the profile, choosing the right categories, filling in every field truthfully, adding real photos and earning genuine reviews. Google says local results rely mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, so each step below is aimed at one of those three.

This guide is for owners of businesses that serve customers in person or in a defined area. Follow the steps in order. Each one says what to do and what to avoid.

## How Does Google Decide Which Businesses to Show?

Google's own help page on [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) names three factors:

| Factor | What it means | What you can influence |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well your profile matches what the person searched for | Categories, services, description, complete details |
| Distance | How far you are from the searcher or the location in the query | Accurate address and service area (you cannot change where the searcher is) |
| Prominence | How well known your business is | Reviews, links, mentions and directory listings across the web |

Google also states that you cannot pay for a better local ranking. That is why the work below is about accuracy and completeness, not tricks.

## How Do You Claim and Verify Your Profile?

Search for your business on Google or Maps first. If a listing already exists, claim it instead of creating a duplicate. If not, create one from the Business Profile manager.

1. Enter your business name exactly as it appears on your sign, invoices and website.
2. Add a real street address if customers visit you. If you travel to customers, set a service area and hide the address.
3. Complete the verification method Google offers for your business.
4. Wait for confirmation before making large edits.

Verification matters because Google's help pages state that photos and other profile content only show publicly once the business is verified. An unverified profile is close to invisible.

## How Do You Choose the Right Categories?

Categories tell Google what you are, and they are the strongest relevance signal you control.

- **Primary category:** pick the one that describes your core business, not your most profitable side line. A mobile windshield repair company chooses a windshield-repair category, not a generic "car service" one.
- **Secondary categories:** add only those that describe real services you offer.
- **Check competitors:** search your main keyword, open the top three profiles, and look at the categories they use. Copy the category when it truly fits, not the wording of their descriptions.

Re-check categories every few months, because Google adds and retires them.

## What Should You Fill In on the Profile?

Fill in every field that applies. A complete profile gives Google more to match against searches.

1. **Business name.** Use your real-world name only. Adding keywords, a city or "best" to the name breaks Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) and can get the profile suspended. Put keywords in the description and services instead.
2. **Phone and website.** Use a local number and link to the page that best fits the location, usually your home page or a location page.
3. **Hours.** Set regular hours and update special hours for holidays.
4. **Services and products.** List each service with a short, plain description.
5. **Description.** Say what you do, who you serve and where, in a few clear sentences. Skip promotions and links.
6. **Attributes.** Turn on the ones that are true, such as wheelchair access or online appointments.

## How Should You Handle Photos and Videos?

Photos make the profile credible and are one of the first things people tap. Google's [photo guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862) says images should be in focus, well lit and not heavily filtered, so they represent reality. Videos can be up to 30 seconds and 75 MB.

A simple set to upload:

- Your logo and a cover photo
- Exterior shots so people can recognize the building
- Interior or workspace shots
- Your team at work
- Finished jobs or products

Use your own photos, not stock images. Add new ones regularly so the profile does not look abandoned.

## How Do You Get More Reviews?

Reviews feed prominence, and they also decide whether a searcher clicks. Google lists review count and score among the signals in local ranking.

- **Ask at the right moment.** Right after a job is finished or a purchase is made.
- **Make it easy.** Send the direct review link by text or email.
- **Reply to every review.** Thank people for good ones. Answer bad ones calmly with facts and an offer to fix the problem.
- **Never buy or invent reviews, and never offer rewards for positive ones.** It breaks Google's policies and can remove your reviews or your listing.

## Should You Post Updates and Answer Questions?

Posts are optional but cheap. Use them for offers, events and news that give a visitor a reason to act. Keep each one short, with a clear button.

Check your profile now and then for new questions and suggested edits from the public. Other people can propose changes to your hours or phone number, so wrong information can appear without you doing anything.

## How Does Your Website Support Your Profile?

The profile and your site work as a pair, and prominence includes what exists across the web.

- Make sure your business name, address and phone number on the site match the profile.
- Give each location or service area its own page with unique content.
- Add `LocalBusiness` structured data that matches the profile. Our [technical SEO audit checklist](/blog/technical-seo-audit-checklist/) explains how to validate schema.
- Keep your listings on major directories consistent with the profile.

## How Do You Measure Whether It Is Working?

In the Business Profile performance view, track:

- Profile views from Search and Maps
- Calls, website clicks and direction requests
- The search terms people used to find you

Review the numbers monthly. Compare against the same period a year earlier if you can, because many local businesses are seasonal. ## What Are the Most Common Mistakes?

These errors show up again and again on profiles that stall:

- **Duplicate listings.** Two profiles for one location split reviews and confuse Google. Merge or remove the extra one through the manager.
- **Wrong primary category.** A category that is too broad or describes a side service sends you into the wrong searches.
- **Outdated hours.** Customers who arrive at a closed door leave bad reviews, and those reviews stay.
- **Ignored reviews.** Unanswered complaints tell the next reader that nobody is paying attention.
- **Inconsistent details.** A different phone number on the profile, the website and directories makes your data harder to trust.
- **One-time setup.** Treat the profile as a living asset. Put a monthly reminder in your calendar to check photos, hours, reviews and questions.

Fixing these usually costs nothing but time, and it is the cheapest local SEO work available to most businesses.

## Google Business Profile Optimization Checklist

1. Profile claimed and verified
2. Real business name, no added keywords
3. Accurate address or service area
4. One primary category and only the true secondary ones
5. Hours, phone and website set
6. Services listed with descriptions
7. At least a logo, cover photo, exterior and interior photos
8. A review request process and replies to every review
9. Website details match the profile
10. Monthly check of performance numbers

## FAQ

### How long does Google Business Profile optimization take?

The initial setup takes a few hours if you have your photos and details ready. Verification can take longer. After that, plan on a short monthly check for reviews, photos, hours and performance.

### Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a profile costs nothing, and Google states that you cannot pay for a better local ranking.

### Can I rank in Maps without a physical storefront?

Yes, if you serve customers at their location. Set a service area and hide your address. You still need to follow Google's guidelines about what counts as a business location.

### Why was my Business Profile suspended?

Common causes are a business name with added keywords, an address that does not qualify, or content that breaks Google's policies. Compare your profile with the [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), fix the issue, then submit a reinstatement request.

### Do I still need a website?

Yes. The profile gets you seen, but your site gives Google more detail about your services and gives customers a place to learn more.
