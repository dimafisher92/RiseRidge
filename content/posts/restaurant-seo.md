Restaurant SEO is the work of making your restaurant or catering business show up when nearby people search for what you serve: "tacos near me", "brunch downtown", "wedding catering [city]". Most of those searches are won in Google's map pack, so the job comes down to a complete Google Business Profile, a menu that search engines can read, a steady flow of genuine reviews, and a fast mobile website that makes booking or ordering easy. The same signals also decide which restaurants ChatGPT and other AI assistants name when someone asks for a recommendation.

This guide is for owners and managers of independent restaurants, cafes, bars and catering companies in the US. It covers what drives local rankings for food businesses, what to fix first, and a weekly routine you can keep up during a busy service schedule.

## What Is Restaurant SEO?

Restaurant SEO is local search engine optimization for food and drink businesses. It covers three areas:

- **Your Google Business Profile:** the listing in Google Maps and the map pack, with your hours, menu, photos, reviews, reservation and ordering links.
- **Your website:** menu pages, location pages, catering or private-event pages, and the technical health that lets Google crawl and trust them.
- **Your reputation around the web:** reviews on Google, Yelp and TripAdvisor, local press, food bloggers and directory listings.

Diners rarely search for a restaurant by name the first time. They search for a dish, a cuisine, an occasion or a place: "ramen near Union Square", "kid-friendly pizza", "rooftop bar open late". Your job is to make sure Google has enough clear, consistent information to connect those searches to you. According to [Ahrefs' restaurant SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/blog/restaurant-seo/), which cites a TouchBistro diner survey, 85% of diners still use menus to pick new restaurants. That makes your menu one of the most important SEO assets you own.

## How Does Google Decide Which Restaurants Show Up?

Google ranks local results on three main factors, summarized in [Ahrefs' guide to Google Maps SEO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-maps-seo/):

| Factor | What it means for a restaurant | What you can control |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well your listing matches the dish, cuisine or occasion searched | Primary and secondary categories, menu items, attributes, website menu and event pages |
| Distance | How close you are to the searcher, or to the place named in the search | Your real address, delivery or catering service area, pages for areas you truly serve |
| Prominence | How well known and trusted your restaurant is | Reviews, photos, press mentions, food blogger links, directory listings |

The same guide reports that, according to Whitespark's 2026 ranking-factor data, your primary category is the #1 ranking factor for Google Maps. "Restaurant" alone is rarely the best choice. "Thai restaurant", "Seafood restaurant", "Caterer" or "Brunch restaurant" tells Google far more about which searches you should appear for.

## How Do You Set Up Your Google Business Profile?

Your profile is the first thing most new guests see. If it is unclaimed or half-finished, start there. Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/) walks through every field; these are the settings that matter most for food businesses.

### Pick the most specific primary category

Choose the category that describes what most guests come for. Add secondary categories only for things you genuinely offer: a "Bar" category if you run a real bar program, "Caterer" if catering is a meaningful part of revenue. A taqueria that also caters events might use "Mexican restaurant" as primary and "Caterer" as secondary.

### Add your menu to the profile

Google lets food businesses add menu sections, items, descriptions and prices directly to the profile, or link to a menu on their website. Do both. Dish names on the profile help you show up for dish-level searches, such as "birria tacos near me", that never mention your restaurant's name. Keep prices current.

### Turn on reservations and ordering

If you take bookings or orders, connect them to the profile so people can act without leaving Google. Third-party reservation and delivery platforms work, but consider adding a direct ordering link as well. Ahrefs, citing Deloitte research, reports that [40% of consumers prefer to order directly from a restaurant's website](https://ahrefs.com/blog/restaurant-seo/) over third-party apps, and direct orders avoid platform commissions.

### Keep hours and attributes accurate

- Update holiday hours before every major holiday, not after the first complaint.
- Fill in attributes that apply: outdoor seating, vegan options, wheelchair access, live music, good for groups.
- Use your real business name. Adding keywords or cities to the name breaks Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended.

### Post real photos, often

Photograph your best dishes, your dining room and your team. Ahrefs notes that Google [recommends adding at least three photos of the food and drink you serve](https://ahrefs.com/blog/restaurant-seo/); most successful restaurant profiles have far more, refreshed whenever the menu or the room changes. Skip stock photos.

## Why Does Your Menu Need to Be an HTML Page, Not a PDF?

A PDF or image menu is the most common website mistake restaurants make. It is hard to read on a phone, slow to load, and gives Google less structured information to work with. Ahrefs' guide makes the same point: a PDF menu forces visitors to pinch and zoom, and the page that ranks may just be a near-empty landing page with a link to the file.

Build your menu as a normal web page instead:

1. Use one heading per section (Starters, Mains, Desserts, Drinks) and list each dish as text with its description and price.
2. Mark dietary information in words: vegan, gluten-free, contains nuts. People search for these terms.
3. If you have several menus (lunch, dinner, brunch, catering), give each its own page with a clear title.
4. Link the menu page from your homepage and your Google profile.
5. Update the page the same day the menu changes, then update the profile to match.

If you must keep a PDF for printing, offer it as a download from the HTML menu page, not as a replacement for it.

## Which Pages Does a Restaurant Website Need?

A restaurant site needs a few pages that each answer a real question clearly.

| Page | What it should answer |
|---|---|
| Homepage | What you serve, where you are, hours, and a clear "Book" or "Order" button |
| Menu page(s) | Every dish as text, with prices and dietary notes |
| Location page | Address, parking, transit, nearby landmarks, map, hours, phone |
| Catering page | What you cater (weddings, offices, parties), minimum order, service area, how to request a quote |
| Private events page | Room capacity, packages, booking steps |
| About page | Who runs the kitchen, the story, sourcing; the details that make you different |

### Catering and event pages are search magnets

Catering searches are high-value and less competitive than "restaurant near me". A dedicated catering page that names the event types you handle and the cities you deliver to can rank for searches like "office lunch catering [city]" or "wedding catering [neighborhood]". If you serve several towns, add a page for each one only when you have something specific to say: delivery times, local venues you work with, popular orders in that area. Pages that only swap the city name tend to perform poorly.

### Target nearby landmarks

Many diners search by place: "restaurants near the convention center". Mention the venues, hotels and attractions within walking distance on your location page, with honest walking times.

### Technical basics

Restaurant sites often lose rankings and bookings to simple problems:

- Heavy image galleries and video headers that make the site slow on phones.
- A phone number that is an image instead of a tap-to-call link.
- Booking or ordering widgets that break on mobile.
- Missing or duplicate page titles and descriptions.
- Old pages for closed locations or past events that now return errors.

## What Structured Data Should a Restaurant Use?

Structured data tells search engines exactly what your business is. Schema.org has a `Restaurant` type (a kind of `FoodEstablishment`, which is a kind of `LocalBusiness`). Useful properties include:

- `name`, `address`, `telephone`, `url` and `geo`
- `openingHoursSpecification` for regular and holiday hours
- `servesCuisine` (for example "Mexican" or "Vietnamese")
- `priceRange`
- `hasMenu`, pointing to your HTML menu page
- `acceptsReservations`

Use `CateringBusiness` or add catering details on the catering page if catering is a separate line of business. Keep every value consistent with what is on the page and on your Google profile. Do not add review or rating markup for your own restaurant; that is not what it is for.

## How Do You Get More Restaurant Reviews?

Reviews influence both where you rank and whether a searcher picks you over the place next door. For restaurants, Google reviews matter most: according to the TouchBistro survey cited in [Ahrefs' guide](https://ahrefs.com/blog/restaurant-seo/), 43% of diners read Google restaurant reviews, compared with 31% on Yelp and 16% on TripAdvisor.

A review routine that fits into service:

1. **Ask at the right moment.** When the check is paid and the guest is happy, a server can mention it. For catering, ask the day after the event.
2. **Make it one tap.** Put a QR code linking to your Google review form on table cards, receipts or takeout bags. Add the link to reservation follow-up emails.
3. **Ask everyone.** Choosing to ask only happy guests ("review gating") breaks Google's policies.
4. **Never pay or discount for reviews.** No free desserts or drinks in exchange for a review. Incentivized reviews break Google's rules and can cost you the reviews you already have.
5. **Reply to every review.** Thank people for specific praise. For complaints, apologize once, state the facts calmly and offer to make it right offline. Future guests read your replies as closely as the reviews.

## How Do Citations and Local Links Help?

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone number (NAP). Google uses them to confirm your details, and AI assistants lean on the same listings. Keep them consistent:

- Make your NAP identical on your website, Google profile, Yelp, TripAdvisor, Apple Maps, Bing Places and the reservation and delivery platforms you use.
- Remove duplicate listings, especially after a move or a name change.

Local links carry more weight than generic ones. Good sources for restaurants include local food bloggers and newspaper food sections, "best of" lists in city guides, your suppliers' "where to find us" pages, neighborhood business associations, and events you sponsor or cater.

## Does AI Search Change Restaurant SEO?

For classic "near me" searches, the map pack is still the main battleground. According to [Ahrefs' research](https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-maps-seo/), only 7.9% of local searches trigger an AI Overview.

AI assistants are a growing second channel, though. People ask ChatGPT or Perplexity "where should I take my parents for dinner in [city]?" or "who caters a 50-person office lunch near me?", and the answers name specific businesses. Those answers are assembled mostly from third-party sources: review sites, directories, local guides and "best of" lists. In [our ChatGPT recommendation experiment](/blog/chatgpt-recommendation-experiment/), which looked at local law firms, every shortlist was built from third-party sources rather than the firms' own websites. Our posts on [law firm SEO in the age of AI search](/blog/law-firm-seo-ai-search/) and [local SEO for auto repair shops](/blog/local-seo-auto-repair-shops/) show the same pattern in other local industries.

To improve your odds of being named:

- Keep your details consistent across every listing.
- Build review volume on Google and the other major review sites.
- Get included in local guides and "best of" lists your city's diners actually read.
- Describe your cuisine, signature dishes and catering services in plain, specific language on your site.

## Weekly Restaurant SEO Checklist

Most of this takes less than an hour a week once the setup is done.

| When | Task |
|---|---|
| Every week | Reply to all new reviews on Google, Yelp and TripAdvisor |
| Every week | Post 3–5 new photos of dishes, specials or the room |
| Every week | Check that hours, menu prices and ordering links still work |
| Every menu change | Update the HTML menu page and the Google profile menu the same day |
| Before holidays | Set holiday hours on Google and every major listing |
| Every month | Ask for reviews consistently and check your review count against nearby competitors |
| Every month | Pitch one local link: a blogger, a city guide, a supplier page, an event |
| Every quarter | Check your map pack position for your main dishes and cuisine, and ask ChatGPT who it recommends for your cuisine in your area |

## FAQ

### How long does restaurant SEO take to work?

Quick fixes such as choosing the right category, adding your menu and fixing hours can show results within a few weeks. Ranking in the map pack for competitive searches like "best pizza [big city]" usually takes several months of steady reviews, photos and links. The more restaurants nearby, the longer it takes.

### Is a PDF menu bad for SEO?

It is worse than an HTML menu. PDFs are hard to read on phones and give Google less clear information about your dishes. Put your menu on a normal web page with each dish as text, and offer the PDF only as an optional download.

### Do I need a website if I'm on Google, Yelp and delivery apps?

Yes. Google compares your profile with your website, and your own site is where you control the menu, catering details and direct ordering. Delivery apps own the customer relationship and charge commissions; your website does not.

### How many Google reviews does a restaurant need?

There is no fixed number. What matters is how you compare with the restaurants people consider alongside you, and whether new reviews keep arriving every week. A steady trickle beats a burst followed by months of silence.

### How do catering companies get found online?

Build a dedicated catering page for each main service (weddings, corporate, private parties), list the areas you deliver to, add "Caterer" as a category on your Google profile, and collect reviews after every event. Catering searches are often less competitive than restaurant searches, so a clear, specific page can rank faster.
