SEO usually costs between $250 and $10,000 per month, and most businesses land between $500 and $5,000, according to [Ahrefs' poll of 439 SEO providers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/). Where you fall in that range depends on the pricing model, your competition, the size of your site and how much of the work you want done for you.

This guide explains the three common pricing models, what pushes a quote up or down, and how to check that a price is fair before you sign.

## How Much Does SEO Cost Per Month?

Monthly retainers are the most common way to buy SEO. In the Ahrefs poll, the most popular retainer band was $501 to $1,000 (20.4% of providers), and 42.8% of providers charged between $501 and $2,000. About 31% charged $2,001 or more.

Averages differ by type of provider:

| Provider type | Average monthly fee (Ahrefs poll) |
|---|---|
| Agency | $3,209 |
| Consultancy | $3,250 |
| Freelancer | $1,348 |

Treat averages as a rough guide. They are estimates built from price bands, and providers in the poll skew toward small and mid-sized clients. For local businesses the same poll puts the average at about $1,557 per month, against about $3,474 worldwide.

## What Are the Common SEO Pricing Models?

There are three models. Each fits a different situation.

### Monthly retainer

You pay a fixed fee each month for ongoing work: technical fixes, content, links, reporting. This suits sites that need continuous improvement, because SEO results build over months rather than days. Ask for a written list of what the retainer includes each month.

### Hourly rate

You pay for time. In the Ahrefs poll, 90% of providers charged $150 per hour or less, and 47% charged between $75 and $200. Hourly pricing works for consulting, a second opinion or a specific question. It is a poor fit for open-ended work, because the bill has no natural ceiling.

### Per-project fee

You pay a set price for a defined deliverable, such as a technical audit, a site migration or a content plan. The most common project band in the poll was $2,501 to $5,000 (21.2% of providers), and 60.6% charged $1,001 or more. Projects are easy to budget but stop when the deliverable ships, so they do not replace ongoing work.

## What Affects the Price of SEO?

Two quotes for "SEO" can describe very different amounts of work. These factors explain most of the gap:

1. **Competition.** Ranking for a term that large brands target takes more content and more authority than ranking for a local service term.
2. **Site size and condition.** A 40-page site with clean code costs less to fix than a 5,000-product store with duplicate URLs.
3. **Scope.** Technical fixes only, content included, local listings, and AI-search visibility are different workloads.
4. **Who does the work.** Poll data shows fees rising with experience: roughly $1,541 per month for providers with two years or less, against about $3,648 for those with five to ten years.
5. **Speed expectations.** Faster timelines need more output, which costs more.

The Ahrefs poll splits some figures by market, which helps you sanity-check a quote against businesses like yours.

## What Costs Do People Forget to Budget For?

The retainer is rarely the whole bill. Plan for these:

- **Tools.** Rank tracking, crawling and analytics software. Some agencies include these, and some pass them through.
- **Content production.** Check whether articles, images and page copy are inside the fee or billed per piece.
- **Development time.** Technical fixes sometimes need a developer on your side, especially on custom sites.
- **Your own time.** Someone has to approve content, share product knowledge and answer questions. Slow approvals slow results.
- **Link or PR spend.** Ask whether outreach costs are included and how links are earned.

Ask each provider to list what is included, what is extra and what you must supply. Comparing quotes line by line is the fastest way to see why two prices differ.

## What Should You Expect in the First Six Months?

Timelines vary, but the work usually moves in this order, which is why the first months can feel quiet:

1. **Month 1:** audit, tracking setup, keyword and page mapping.
2. **Months 2 to 3:** technical fixes and the first new or rewritten pages go live.
3. **Months 3 to 6:** pages get indexed and start to rank, and the team doubles down on what is working.

Because results lag spending, avoid judging the first 30 days on traffic alone. Judge them on whether the agreed work shipped. If you want an example of what the early work looks like, read our [SEO case studies lessons learned](/blog/seo-case-studies-lessons-learned/).

## Is Cheap SEO Worth It?

Sometimes, for a small and simple site, a freelancer at the low end of the range is enough. The risk is in the very cheap, fixed-price "guaranteed rankings" offers. Nobody outside Google controls its rankings, so any promise of a guaranteed position should make you cautious. Ask what is actually delivered each month and how it will be measured. If the answer is vague, the price is not the bargain it looks like.

## How Do You Know If an SEO Quote Is Fair?

Run any quote through these checks:

1. **Is the scope written down?** Deliverables per month, not "ongoing optimization."
2. **Is the contract flexible?** Month-to-month terms let you leave if results stall. RiseRidge works month-to-month with no long-term lock-in.
3. **Who does the work?** Ask whether the people on the call are the people doing the work.
4. **How is success measured?** Look for organic traffic, leads and revenue, not only rankings.
5. **Is there proof?** Ask for case studies with real numbers. Ours are on the [case studies page](/case-studies/), for example a mobile auto-glass shop in Charlotte, NC whose average Google position moved from 15.5 to 10.1 in the first 90 days.

## When Does SEO Pay Back?

SEO is an investment that compounds. A good test is to compare the monthly fee against the value of one new customer. If a retainer of $1,500 brings in two customers worth $2,000 each, it covers itself. ## FAQ

### How much should a small business spend on SEO?

Many small businesses spend between $500 and $2,000 per month. Ahrefs' poll found 42% of providers charge $250 to $1,000 per month for small business SEO. Start with the budget you can sustain for at least six months, because results take time.

### Is SEO cheaper than paid ads?

Per click, usually yes over time, because organic traffic does not charge for each visit. But SEO takes months to build, while ads send traffic the day you switch them on. Many businesses run both.

### Why do SEO agencies charge so much?

Agency fees cover strategy, technical work, content, reporting and account management, often from several specialists. If you are weighing that against a salaried hire, see our comparison of [an SEO agency vs. in-house SEO](/blog/seo-agency-vs-in-house/). The Ahrefs poll shows agency averages well above freelancer averages for that reason. The right question is what you get for the fee.

### Can I do SEO myself for free?

Yes, for the basics. Google's free Search Console and a checklist like our [Shopify SEO checklist](/blog/shopify-seo-checklist/) cover a lot. The cost is your time, and the learning curve on technical issues.

### How long before I see results from paying for SEO?

Expect early movement within a few months and stronger gains after that. The timing depends on competition and site health. A provider promising page one in weeks should explain how.
