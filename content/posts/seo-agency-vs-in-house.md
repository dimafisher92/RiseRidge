An SEO agency gives you a team of specialists for a monthly fee, while an in-house SEO is an employee who knows your business deeply but covers fewer skills on their own. Agencies usually win on speed and breadth of expertise; in-house hires win on product knowledge and day-to-day control. Many growing companies end up with a hybrid: one internal owner plus an agency for the specialist work.

This guide compares the two options on cost, skills, speed and control, explains when each one fits, and gives you a checklist to make the call for your own business.

## What Is the Difference Between an SEO Agency and In-House SEO?

**In-house SEO** means the work is done by people on your payroll. That can be one dedicated specialist, a marketer who handles SEO alongside other jobs, or a full team. As Ahrefs' Patrick Stox puts it in his piece on [the realities of in-house SEO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/in-house-seo/), companies hire in-house when they want someone "invested in the long-term success of the company" who can work closely with other teams.

**An SEO agency** is an outside firm you pay to plan and carry out the work. A typical engagement covers technical fixes, content, local listings, links and reporting, delivered by several people with different specialties.

The core tasks are the same either way. What changes is who does them, how many skills you can draw on, and how the cost is structured.

## SEO Agency vs. In-House: Side-by-Side Comparison

| Factor | SEO agency | In-house SEO |
|---|---|---|
| Cost structure | Monthly retainer or project fee | Salary, benefits, tools and management time |
| Range of skills | Several specialists (technical, content, local, links) | Limited to what one person or team knows |
| Speed to start | Weeks | Months, once you include hiring and onboarding |
| Business knowledge | Learned over time from you | Deep, day to day |
| Control over priorities | Shared, set by contract and scope | Full |
| Access to developers and other teams | Through your point of contact | Direct |
| Risk if a person leaves | Agency reassigns the account | Work stops until you rehire |
| Tools | Often included in the fee | You buy and maintain them |
| Flexibility | Scale up, down or cancel per contract terms | Harder to change headcount |

Neither column is better across the board. The right choice depends on which rows matter most for your business right now.

## How Much Does an SEO Agency Cost Compared With an In-House Hire?

The honest answer is that the two are hard to compare line by line, because one is a fee and the other is a salary plus overhead. Here are the best public numbers we could verify.

**Agency fees.** In [Ahrefs' poll of 439 SEO providers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/), agencies charged $3,209 per month on average, against $1,348 for freelancers and $3,250 for consultancies. Most businesses spend between $500 and $5,000 per month. For local SEO, agencies averaged $1,819 per month. Our guide to [how much SEO costs](/blog/how-much-does-seo-cost/) breaks these figures down by pricing model.

**In-house salaries.** [Ahrefs' 2024 SEO salary survey](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-salary/) of 234 SEOs found a median annual salary of $58,288. Median salaries by role were:

| Role | Median annual salary (USD) |
|---|---|
| SEO Specialist | $45,090 |
| SEO Manager | $58,107 |
| Head of SEO | $81,522 |
| SEO Director | $97,468 |

One caveat matters here: most responses were collected at a UK conference, so the survey says it mainly reflects UK salaries. Check salary data for your own city before you budget a US hire.

### A simple cost comparison

Take the survey's median SEO Manager salary of $58,107. That works out to about $4,842 per month before benefits, payroll taxes, tools, training and the time someone spends managing the role. An agency charging the poll's average of $3,209 per month costs about $38,508 a year, usually with tools included.

On paper the agency looks cheaper, but this is not the full picture:

- One in-house person cannot cover technical SEO, content, local and link building at the same depth a team can.
- An agency still needs time from your side: approvals, product knowledge and developer help.
- A larger site often needs both, which changes the math again.

Run the comparison with your own numbers: the salary you would actually pay, plus the overhead, against two or three real agency quotes.

## When Does an SEO Agency Make More Sense?

An agency is usually the better fit when:

1. **You need results to start soon.** Hiring and onboarding a good SEO takes months. An agency can start an audit within weeks.
2. **You need several skills at once.** A site with technical problems, thin content and a weak Google Business Profile needs three kinds of expertise. One hire rarely has all three.
3. **Your budget is below a full salary.** If you can spend a few thousand dollars a month but not a full-time salary, a retainer gets more done for the money.
4. **SEO is not your core business.** A dental practice or a law firm wants leads, not an SEO department to manage.
5. **You want to test SEO before committing.** A month-to-month agency contract lets you stop if results do not come. RiseRidge, for example, works month-to-month with no long-term lock-in.

Agencies also see many sites, which helps with fast-moving changes such as AI search. ## When Does In-House SEO Make More Sense?

Build in-house when:

1. **SEO is a main growth channel.** If organic search drives a large share of revenue, you want someone thinking about it every day.
2. **Your site is large and changes often.** Big e-commerce catalogs, marketplaces and publishers ship changes weekly. An internal SEO can review them before they go live.
3. **You need close work with developers and product.** SEO problems are often engineering problems. An employee in the sprint planning meeting catches them early.
4. **Your product is complex.** Deep technical or regulated products take a long time to learn. An in-house person builds that knowledge and keeps it.
5. **You can afford the full stack.** That means the salary, the tools and enough budget for content or development help.

The main risk is concentration. If your one SEO leaves, the work stops and the knowledge walks out with them. Document processes and keep access to tools in company accounts, not personal ones.

## What Is the Hybrid Model?

The hybrid model pairs an internal SEO owner with an outside agency. It is often the most practical setup for mid-sized companies.

A common split looks like this:

| In-house owner | Agency |
|---|---|
| Sets priorities and owns results | Runs audits and technical fixes |
| Knows the product, customers and brand voice | Produces content at volume |
| Works with developers to ship changes | Handles links, digital PR and local listings |
| Reviews and approves agency work | Brings data from many sites and new tactics |

This works well when the internal person is a marketer who understands SEO but does not have time to do everything. The agency adds hands and specialist skills; the internal owner keeps the work aligned with the business. It also reduces the risk of a single person leaving.

For the hybrid model to work, agree on who decides what. The in-house owner should set goals and approve changes. The agency should report against those goals, not against its own list of tasks.

## How Long Does Each Option Take to Show Results?

SEO results take time whichever way you staff it. In [Ahrefs' polls of 3,680 people](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/), the most common answer was three to six months, and the article stresses that the real answer depends on your site's history, your competition and your resources.

What differs is the starting line:

- **Agency:** work begins once the contract is signed. The first month is usually an audit, tracking setup and a plan, followed by fixes and new pages.
- **In-house:** add the time it takes to write a job description, interview, hire and onboard. A new employee also needs time to learn your site and get access to systems.

If you need visible progress this quarter, that head start matters. If you are building for the next three years, it matters less than having the right person.

## How Do You Decide? A 7-Question Checklist

Answer these questions honestly. If most answers point one way, you have your choice.

1. **Is organic search one of your top two revenue channels?** Yes points to in-house or hybrid.
2. **Can you pay a full salary plus tools and overhead for at least two years?** No points to an agency.
3. **Do you need technical, content and local work at the same time?** Yes points to an agency or hybrid.
4. **Does your site change weekly, with developers shipping often?** Yes points to in-house or hybrid.
5. **Do you need results within six months?** Yes points to an agency.
6. **Is there someone internally who could own SEO part-time?** Yes makes hybrid realistic.
7. **Have you tested SEO before?** No points to an agency on a flexible contract first, so you learn what works before you hire.

## What Should You Ask Before Hiring an SEO Agency?

If the checklist points to an agency, vet it carefully:

- **Who does the work?** Ask whether the people in the sales call will work on your account. RiseRidge uses a senior-only team for this reason.
- **What is delivered each month?** Get a written scope, not "ongoing optimization."
- **How is success measured?** Look for leads, sales and organic traffic, not rankings alone.
- **What are the contract terms?** Long lock-ins with no exit are a warning sign.
- **Can they show real results?** Ask for case studies with numbers. On our [case studies page](/case-studies/) you can see, for example, how a mobile auto-glass shop in Charlotte, NC moved its average Google position from 15.5 to 10.1 in its first 90 days.

Be wary of anyone who guarantees a specific ranking. Nobody outside Google controls its results.

## FAQ

### Is it cheaper to hire an SEO agency or an in-house SEO?

For most small and mid-sized businesses, an agency costs less than a full-time hire once you add benefits, tools and management time. Ahrefs' poll puts the average agency retainer at $3,209 per month, while its 2024 salary survey found a median SEO Manager salary of $58,107 a year. Large companies with a lot of SEO work can reach a point where a team is more cost-effective.

### Can a small business do SEO in-house?

Yes, for the basics. An owner or marketer can handle Google Business Profile updates, simple on-page fixes and regular content. Technical problems and competitive markets are where outside help usually pays off.

### What is a hybrid SEO model?

It is a setup where an internal employee owns SEO strategy and priorities, and an agency handles specialist or high-volume work such as technical audits, content production and link building. It gives you business knowledge and a wider skill set at the same time.

### Should I hire an SEO agency or a freelancer?

A freelancer suits a narrow, well-defined job, such as a one-off audit or monthly content. Ahrefs' poll shows freelancers average $1,348 per month, well below agencies. An agency suits work that needs several skills at once or more capacity than one person has.

### How do I switch from an agency to in-house SEO?

Start by hiring the internal owner while the agency is still working, so they can learn the account. Make sure all tools, analytics and Search Console access sit in company accounts. Then move work over one area at a time, beginning with the tasks that need the most business knowledge.
