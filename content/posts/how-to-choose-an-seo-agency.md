To choose an SEO agency, decide what business result you need first, then shortlist three agencies and put the same questions to each one: who does the work, what gets delivered every month, how success is measured, and what the contract lets you do if it isn't working. Pick the agency whose answers are specific, written down and tied to revenue or leads, not the one with the boldest promise.

This guide gives you 12 questions to ask, what a good answer sounds like, the red flags that should end the conversation, and a scorecard for comparing your shortlist side by side.

## What Should You Decide Before You Talk to Any SEO Agency?

Agencies are good at selling. You will compare them more fairly if you walk in with three things already settled.

**1. The business goal.** "More traffic" is not a goal. Write down the result that matters: more booked calls, more online orders, more first-time customers, more quote requests from a specific city. Every question below gets easier when the agency has to answer against that goal.

**2. Your budget range.** SEO pricing varies a lot. In [Ahrefs' poll of 439 SEO providers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/), agencies charged $3,209 per month on average, against roughly $1,349 for freelancers. Our guide to [how much SEO costs](/blog/how-much-does-seo-cost/) explains the pricing models in detail. Knowing your range keeps you from wasting calls on agencies built for a different budget.

**3. Whether you need an agency at all.** If organic search is already a core channel and your site ships changes every week, an in-house hire or a hybrid setup may suit you better. Our comparison of [SEO agency vs. in-house SEO](/blog/seo-agency-vs-in-house/) walks through that decision.

Then gather the access an agency will ask for: Google Search Console, Google Analytics, your CMS or Shopify admin, and Google Business Profile if you serve a local area. An agency that never asks for data before quoting is guessing.

## How Do You Build an SEO Agency Shortlist?

Three agencies is enough to compare. More than five and the calls start to blur together. Good places to find candidates:

- **Referrals from businesses like yours.** Ask peers in your industry who they use and whether they would hire them again.
- **Agencies that show up for the work they sell.** An SEO agency that can't rank or get cited for its own services is a weak sign.
- **AI assistants.** Ask ChatGPT or Perplexity to recommend SEO agencies for your type of business, then check the sources it cites. If you care about AI search, an agency that shows up there for itself has done the work at least once.
- **Case study pages with real numbers.** Skip agencies whose "results" page is a list of logos.

Cut anyone who reaches out with an unsolicited email promising page one. Good agencies are busy enough not to cold-pitch guarantees.

## 12 Questions to Ask an SEO Agency

Ask every agency the same questions, in the same order, and write the answers down. The value is in the comparison.

### 1. Who will actually work on my account?

You want names, roles and seniority. Ask whether the people on the sales call will touch your site after you sign. A common pattern is a senior pitch followed by hand-off to junior staff. RiseRidge uses a senior-only team for exactly this reason, but whoever you hire, get the answer in writing.

### 2. What will you deliver in the first 90 days?

A good agency can describe its first three months without hedging: an audit, tracking setup, a prioritized fix list, then the first round of changes and new pages. "We'll start optimizing" is not a plan. Ask to see a sample audit or a redacted 90-day plan from another client.

### 3. What exactly do I get each month?

Ask for a written scope with quantities: pages rewritten, technical fixes shipped, articles published, listings managed. To show what that looks like, here is the work log from the first 40 days of one of our Shopify engagements: 38 title tags, 186 meta descriptions, alt text on 210 images and Product structured data on 175 pages. You should be able to check deliverables like these on your own site.

### 4. How will you measure success?

The right answer names business metrics first: leads, orders, revenue from organic search, then the supporting numbers (non-brand clicks, rankings for buying-intent terms). Rankings alone are a vanity metric if they don't bring buyers. Ask which dashboard you will see and how often.

### 5. What results have you gotten for a business like mine?

Ask for a case study in your industry or business model, with before-and-after numbers and a time frame. On our [case studies page](/case-studies/), for example, a DTC accessories brand on Shopify grew organic sales by 24.5% in its first 40 days. Then ask a follow-up most buyers skip: "What did that client's site look like before you started?" Results from a broken site are easier to get than results from a healthy one.

### 6. How do you handle technical SEO, and who makes the changes?

Many SEO problems are development problems: slow pages, duplicate URLs, indexing errors, broken structured data. Ask whether the agency implements fixes itself or hands you a list. If it hands you a list, you need developer time budgeted. Our [technical SEO audit checklist](/blog/technical-seo-audit-checklist/) shows what a thorough audit should cover, so you can judge the sample they send.

### 7. How do you approach content?

Ask who writes it, who checks facts and how topics are chosen. If AI tools are part of the process, ask what human review happens before anything is published. Thin, unreviewed AI content is a risk to your site, not a shortcut. Look for an agency that plans content around the questions your buyers actually ask.

### 8. What about links?

Ask how they earn links and mentions, and ask them to show examples. Be wary of anyone who sells a fixed number of links per month or talks about "link packages." Paid link schemes can get a site penalized, and you, not the agency, carry that risk.

### 9. Do you work on AI search visibility?

Buyers now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews for recommendations. Ask whether the agency tracks if your brand is named or cited in those answers and what it does to improve that. If they don't know what [generative engine optimization](/blog/what-is-generative-engine-optimization/) is, they are behind. At RiseRidge this is our [Rank on AI service](/rank-on-ai/), which monitors ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity.

### 10. Who owns the accounts, content and data?

Everything should sit in your company's accounts: Search Console, Analytics, your CMS, Google Business Profile, any tools set up for you. Content written for you should belong to you. If you leave, you should keep all of it without a fight.

### 11. What are the contract terms?

Ask about minimum length, notice period and what happens to work in progress if you cancel. SEO takes time: in [Ahrefs' polls of 3,680 people](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/), the most common answer was three to six months before results show. A fair agency asks for enough time to do the work but doesn't lock you into a year with no exit. RiseRidge works month-to-month with no long-term lock-in.

### 12. Do you work with my direct competitors?

Some agencies refuse to take two clients competing for the same keywords in the same market. Others do it openly. Either can be fine, but you should know before you share your strategy and data.

## What Red Flags Should Rule Out an SEO Agency?

Any one of these is reason to walk away:

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Guaranteed rankings or "page one in 30 days" | Nobody outside Google controls Google's results. |
| Secret methods they won't explain | Good SEO is not secret. Hidden tactics are often the risky ones. |
| A fixed number of backlinks for a fee | Bought links can trigger a penalty that you have to clean up. |
| Reports that show only rankings and traffic | Hides whether the work brings in customers. |
| The agency owns your accounts or content | You lose your data and work if you leave. |
| Long contracts with no exit clause | Removes their reason to keep earning your business. |
| No questions about your business before the quote | The quote isn't based on your site or goals. |
| Prices far below the market | Usually means automated work or no real work at all. |

## What Should Happen After You Sign?

The first month tells you a lot. A good agency will:

1. **Ask for access straight away** and confirm what tracking is in place.
2. **Deliver an audit with priorities**, not a 200-page export from a tool.
3. **Agree on the metrics** you set out in your goal, and set a baseline.
4. **Ship the first fixes within weeks**, so you can see changes on your own site.
5. **Report in plain language**: what was done, what changed, what comes next.

If you reach the end of month two with no shipped work and no clear plan, raise it. If it doesn't change, use the exit you negotiated.

## FAQ

### How many SEO agencies should I talk to before choosing one?

Three is usually enough to compare. Ask each one the same questions so you can line the answers up side by side, and score them on the same criteria. Talking to many more tends to make the choice harder, not better.

### How much should I pay an SEO agency?

It depends on your competition, your site's size and the scope of work. In Ahrefs' poll of 439 providers, agencies averaged $3,209 per month. Be careful with quotes far below market rates, because they usually mean less real work.

### Is it a red flag if an SEO agency guarantees first-page rankings?

Yes. No agency controls Google's results, so a ranking guarantee is either a sales tactic or a sign of risky methods. A trustworthy agency will tell you what it will do and what results it has reached for similar clients, and will measure progress against your business goals.

### How long should I give an SEO agency before judging results?

The most common answer in Ahrefs' polls of 3,680 people was three to six months for SEO to show results. You should still see shipped work, such as fixes and new pages, within the first few weeks. Judge the work early and the results over a few months.

### Should I choose a local SEO agency or a national one?

Location matters less than experience with businesses like yours. If you serve a local area, look for proven Google Business Profile and local landing page work; our guide to [Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/) shows what that involves. A remote agency can do this well if it understands your market.
